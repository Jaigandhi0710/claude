What does a mortgage forensic expert witness do?

A mortgage forensic expert witness examines the records behind a mortgage loan, its servicing history and its chain of title, and reports what those records show. Marie McDonnell, CFE, MAFF, performs this work in three stages: diagnostic, forensic and expositive. Her findings are presented in expert affidavits and reports, and in testimony when required. Her opinions are limited to the records examined; legal conclusions are left to the court.

What records does she examine?

Her examinations follow her service areas: mortgage origination and servicing records; financial records in a financial forensics examination; land and title records in a forensic title examination; and trust and fiduciary financial records. The specific documents reviewed depend on the issues in each matter and are identified in her report. [CONFIRM WITH MARIE: typical document types to list here]

When do attorneys retain her?

Attorneys retain her when a matter depends on mortgage origination, loan servicing, title, or trust and fiduciary records that require forensic analysis. An engagement can begin with a Case Opening Review of the available records and can extend to expert affidavits, reports and testimony. She also serves courts, mediators, arbitrators, public officials and consumers, nationwide, including in Massachusetts foreclosure matters.

How is this different from a "forensic loan audit"?

"Forensic loan audit" is a marketplace term applied to products of widely varying scope. This engagement is defined differently: it is performed by a Certified Fraud Examiner and Master Analyst in Financial Forensics, follows a stated diagnostic, forensic and expositive method, is directed at a specific dispute, and produces expert affidavits and reports prepared for courts, mediators and arbitrators. Does she give legal conclusions?

No. Her Certified Fraud Examiner code prohibits expressing opinions on the guilt or innocence of any person and on legal conclusions. Her reports and testimony describe what the documents and financial records show, the analysis performed, and the basis for each finding. Whether those findings support a legal claim or defense is a question for counsel, the trier of fact and the court.
